Library Examples.BBSSharpIns

BBS# anonymous credentials at P-256

This file instantiates BBS# (Crypto/BBSSharp.v and Backend/BBSSharpVerifier.v) at NIST P-256 (secp256r1, the curve of the paper's implementation), over the curve group of Curve/P256.v with Z/nZ as scalar field. Both sides are exported: the Issuer's and holder's computations (the MAC, its randomisation and the three real provers) and the verifier, so that the OCaml driver Executable/Bbssharpverifier can produce presentations with the extracted prover and check them with the extracted verifier. There are no published BBS# test vectors. The hash-to-scalar oracles and the derivation of the generators are done by the driver and are trusted. The file is extracted by src/Extraction/BBSSharplib.

How a BBS# credential works

BBS# (Desmoulins, Dumanois, Kane and Traoré, ePrint 2025/619) is a pairing-free variant of BBS anonymous credentials, so that the holder's key can live in the secure element of a phone, and its showings are unlinkable.
The credential is an algebraic MAC. With generators g, g̃, g₀, g₁ … g_n, the Issuer's key x (pk_I = g̃^x), the holder's key pk = g^sk and attributes mᵢ: Cm = g₀·pk·∏ gᵢ^mᵢ, A = Cm^(1/(x+e)), and the identity B = Cm·A^(-e) = A^x. To show it, the holder picks fresh r, r₁, r₂ and computes pk_Blind = g^(sk+r), Ā = A^(r₁r₂), D = Cm^r₂, B̄ = Ā^(-e)·D^r₁ = Ā^x and r₃ = r₂⁻¹. Three proofs are attached:
  • validity: one Okamoto statement in G² with witness (-e, r₁, r₃, r, (-mᵢ)_(i∉D)) for B̄ = Ā^(-e)·D^r₁ and g₀·pk_Blind·∏_(i∈D) gᵢ^mᵢ = D^r₃·g^r·∏_(i∉D) gᵢ^(-mᵢ);
  • holder binding: a Schnorr proof for pk_Blind = g^(sk+r);
  • the replacement of the pairing check: a Chaum–Pedersen proof by the Issuer of B̄ = Ā^x ∧ pk_I = g̃^x.
Crypto/BBSSharp.v proves the algebra of the MAC and its randomisation, completeness, special soundness and honest-verifier zero knowledge of the three proofs, the extraction argument (a validity witness together with the Issuer's discrete logarithm gives a MAC on the blinded key and the attributes, or the Issuer's key), and the statistical anonymity of the randomised credential.
